## Background

In August 2029, giant monsters known as kaiju began emerging from the Pacific Ocean
without warning and attacking major metropolitan areas along the West Coast of the
United States and Canada.

The first attacks caught everyone by surprise, but the response recovered quickly.
Regional response centers, staffed by military and civilian personnel, now deploy field
response units: military, emergency services, evacuation teams, and infrastructure
crews. The more notice of an attack, the better.

To provide that notice, the United States and Canada created the Pacific Kaiju Early
Warning Network. No single sensor can reliably detect every kaiju. Some travel
underwater, some surface briefly, and others disrupt electronics. The network combines
observations from sea, land, air, space, and civilian infrastructure, and it maintains a
registry of kaiju to help identify them and understand what they can do.

More signals have not produced more clarity. The response centers are drowning in data.
Multiple analysts manually assemble and interpret the different signals so that the
incident commander can make life-or-death decisions. The observations are there. A
unified picture of what is happening is not.

Apex Dynamics Response Systems was asked to create a unified experience that brings all
available data together visually, helping regional response centers make the best
possible decisions and protect the largest number of lives. This call was the first
joint conversation about that request.

## Transcript

**00:00 | Bontle:** Thank you for joining. Six months ago none of us had a plan for
kaiju. Now we have regional response centers and an early warning network that
collects more than any one desk can read. We have heard the phrase "unified operating
picture" from three regional centers. Before Apex turns that into a proposal, we need
to understand what is breaking today.

**00:24 | Frans:** During the last offshore event I had six displays, two radio nets,
and an analyst standing behind me with a paper map. Everyone had information. Nobody
could tell me whether we were discussing one animal or two.

**00:45 | Kadijah:** What decision were you trying to make at that moment?

**00:49 | Frans:** Whether to move the coastal evacuation boundary and whether to
commit the heavy units. We had enough evidence to worry and not enough agreement to
act cleanly.

**01:11 | Sabien:** Dispatch had already started staging buses. We did not know command
was still debating the track. Our screen said "probable landfall." The marine desk
meant probable within twelve hours. The city desk read it as imminent.

**01:34 | Amalia:** That label came from a legacy source. The early warning network
collects feeds. It does not make the feeds mean the same thing.

**01:44 | Bratislav:** Is inconsistent terminology the main issue?

**01:49 | Amalia:** It is one issue. Timing is another. A buoy can be healthy and still
send an old observation. A satellite pass can be precise and twenty minutes behind.
An empty field can mean no detection, a communications gap, or a sensor type that
never reports that attribute.

**02:18 | John:** From the field, the map was wrong about the bridge approach. The
track could have been perfect and the deployment still would have failed.

**02:29 | Frans:** That bridge closure reached me eight minutes later. I am not
asking a screen to command the incident. I am asking not to spend the first ten
minutes rebuilding the same story from fragments.

**02:43 | Kadijah:** What does "the same story" need to contain?

**02:48 | Frans:** Where it is, where it may be going, how bad it could be, what is
already in motion, and what we do not know.

**03:02 | Sabien:** I would add what changed. A beautiful current picture does not help
me explain why the incoming shift should reverse a decision made ten minutes ago.

**03:14 | Amalia:** I would add where each claim came from. If Apex creates one answer
without showing that the sources disagree, you will make the uncertainty harder to
see.

**03:26 | Bontle:** The request we received was to bring the available data together
visually. Are we still in that territory, or is this an operating-model project?

**03:37 | Frans:** Both, probably. But if you wait to fix the entire operating model,
we will still be using screenshots and radio calls next month.

**03:47 | John:** A shared picture could help. It could also be one more thing the
command center asks us to update while we are moving people out.

**04:02 | Bratislav:** What is the smallest thing we could test without pretending to
solve all of that?

**04:09 | Sabien:** Take a recorded incident and put the scattered pieces in one place.
Then ask a real shift to reconstruct it. Do not show us a perfect feed.

**04:23 | Amalia:** Keep the contradictory readings and missing identifiers. If the
sample is cleaned first, the test will answer the wrong question.

**04:34 | Frans:** Give me something I can scan from across the room. During an
escalation I am not reading a report.

**04:43 | John:** And do not use a dot on a map as proof that the road underneath it
is usable.

**05:02 | Kadijah:** I am hearing at least three jobs: make the current situation
scannable, make the evidence inspectable, and make coordination visible. Are those
actually one experience?

**05:14 | Sabien:** Not for me. My first question is usually, "What needs attention
before the next radio call?"

**05:22 | Frans:** Mine is, "What decision can I no longer delay?"

**05:30 | Amalia:** Mine is, "Which source changed, failed, or contradicted another
source?"

**05:37 | John:** Mine is, "Will the assignment get my crew trapped?"

**05:46 | Bontle:** That is useful. We also have a steering review in early June. They
will not fund a year of integration work from a process diagram. They need to see the
idea.

**06:03 | Kadijah:** A demonstration and a workflow test may need different levels of
fidelity.

**06:08 | Bontle:** Agreed, but we may only get budget for one artifact.

**06:17 | Bratislav:** We can document that tension rather than resolve it today.

**06:25 | Frans:** One more point. The system should not make the decision for me.
If it gives me a threat condition, I need to know that it is a summary, not authority.

**06:39 | Amalia:** Especially if the inputs are incomplete.

**06:44 | Sabien:** Especially at shift change.

**06:49 | John:** Especially when the field knows something the network does not.

**07:02 | Kadijah:** We will follow up with individual interviews and one messy replay
sample. We will treat "unified visual experience" as a starting hypothesis, not a
validated answer.

## Unresolved at close

* Whether the primary user is command, dispatch, data operations, or a shared team
* Whether one view can support rapid scanning and evidence inspection
* How uncertainty should remain visible without increasing cognitive load
* Whether resource coordination belongs in the concept
* Whether a fundable demonstration can also produce valid workflow learning
