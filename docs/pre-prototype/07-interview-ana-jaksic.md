## Interview details

* Participant: Ana Jaksic
* Role: Community representative
* Interviewer: Kadijah Batlouni, Apex service designer
* Date: February 16, 2030
* Duration: 33 minutes

## Transcript excerpt

**Kadijah:** What do response centers misunderstand about community information?

**Ana:** They often count reports as need. A dense neighborhood with working service
produces calls, posts, camera feeds, and transit alerts. An island community with one
damaged tower may produce silence. The map can make the first place look urgent and the
second place look empty.

**Kadijah:** Can you give me an example?

**Ana:** During the second coastal attack, one district generated hundreds of public
signals. A fishing community farther north generated three radio messages through a
volunteer network. Resources followed the volume. Later we learned the smaller
community had lost its dock and evacuation route.

**Kadijah:** Were the response centers aware of the coverage difference?

**Ana:** Individuals were. The combined picture was not. Local coordinators kept
saying, "No data is not no people," but that warning lived in meeting notes.

**Kadijah:** What would you want decision-makers to understand?

**Ana:** Where evidence is weak because observation is weak. Which populations need
more time or different transportation. Whether an evacuation message actually reached
people in a usable form.

**Kadijah:** Would a map help?

**Ana:** A map can reveal distance, routes, and isolation. It can also make everything
that lacks a marker disappear. The question is what earns a marker.

**Kadijah:** What worries you about a severity score?

**Ana:** Severity to whom? A kaiju can be farther away and still be more dangerous to
a community with one road, no hospital, and limited warning coverage.

**Kadijah:** The initial concept is a regional command-center view. Is that the right
place to start?

**Ana:** It is a place to start. Command has power to act. But if all interviews come
from people in the command center, the tool will optimize what they can see already.

**Kadijah:** What would success look like?

**Ana:** Silence prompts a question instead of reassurance, and response priorities
reflect consequence rather than data volume.

**Kadijah:** What should Apex avoid?

**Ana:** Using public social signals as a neutral measure of impact. The loudest data
source is not always the place in greatest danger.

## Researcher note

Ana challenges a signal-rich definition of situational awareness. Her concerns are
