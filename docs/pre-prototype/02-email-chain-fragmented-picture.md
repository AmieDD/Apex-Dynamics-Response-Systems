You asked what a dispatcher has open during a developing event. On my last surge shift:

* regional incident queue
* marine sensor portal
* weather and tide page
* unit-status board
* city closure map
* shared incident chat
* radio log
* a spreadsheet made by the outgoing shift

The spreadsheet was the fastest summary and the least trustworthy record. It contained
copied times with no time zone, color coding nobody could explain, and one monster name
that did not match the registry. We still used it because the official systems did not
show a combined sequence.

The recurring problem is not finding a signal. It is deciding which signals describe
the same event and whether somebody already acted on them.

Some context on why the sources do not line up:

* Underwater arrays report bearings, not a named kaiju.
* Satellite imagery arrives in passes, not continuously.
* Civilian power and transit anomalies can be early signals, but they are noisy.
* Aircraft observations are precise when available and absent during unsafe weather.
* Two agencies use different class labels for the same registry entry.
* Kaiju that disrupt electronics create missing data where risk may be highest.

I can provide a sanitized replay with these problems preserved. It contains a likely
duplicate track, a late correction, and six minutes of silence from one source.

Please do not label the silence "all clear."

Amalia
