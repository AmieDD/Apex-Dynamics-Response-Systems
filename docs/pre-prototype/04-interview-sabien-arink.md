## Interview details

* Participant: Sabien Arink
* Role: Dispatch operations representative
* Interviewer: Kadijah Batlouni, Apex service designer
* Date: February 13, 2030
* Duration: 36 minutes

## Transcript excerpt

**Kadijah:** Show me what happens when a new kaiju report reaches your desk.

**Sabien:** First I check whether it belongs to an incident already open. Names are not
reliable at that point. I compare time, location, movement, and the reporting source.
If it looks credible, I alert the duty lead and start checking resources.

**Kadijah:** What makes that difficult?

**Sabien:** The first report is rarely complete, and every follow-up arrives somewhere
different. The map might update before the incident queue. The radio might have the
best field correction. A manager may message me directly and assume the whole desk saw
it.

**Kadijah:** What are you trying to know about resources?

**Sabien:** Not only how many exist. Whether they are staffed, equipped for the kaiju
class, reachable, already promised elsewhere, and allowed to cross the jurisdiction.
The unit board mostly answers the first question.

**Kadijah:** What happens during a surge?

**Sabien:** I stop reading everything. I watch for changes that require action. New
operators watch every feed and drown. Experienced operators create filters in their
heads, but those filters disappear at handoff.

**Kadijah:** Tell me about a difficult handoff.

**Sabien:** I inherited an incident at 02:00. The previous dispatcher said, "North units
are covered." The board showed assignments, so I moved on. Ten minutes later we learned
that one team had never acknowledged and another was waiting for route clearance.

**Kadijah:** What did "assigned" mean in the board?

**Sabien:** An owner field had been populated. That is not the same as received, accepted,
moving, or on scene. The interface used one calm word for five operational states.

**Kadijah:** What would have prevented the misunderstanding?

**Sabien:** A better handoff, perhaps. A clearer state, perhaps. Fewer systems, perhaps.
I do not want to answer with a feature when the ownership practice is also broken.

**Kadijah:** How would you react to a single regional dashboard?

**Sabien:** If it replaces reconstruction work, good. If it adds a screen that I must
keep current for command, bad. If it shows a recommendation without who confirmed it,
dangerous.

**Kadijah:** What must remain possible if the dashboard fails?

**Sabien:** Radio, paper, and the ability to hand the role to another person. Also the
ability to tell that the dashboard failed. A frozen screen can look very composed.

**Kadijah:** What should a prototype help us learn?

**Sabien:** Whether people notice the right change under load. Put late messages,
duplicates, and an unacknowledged assignment into the test. Do not ask whether the
screen looks clear while nothing is happening.

## Researcher note

Sabien values change detection and handoff continuity more than a complete static
picture. She resists any concept that creates an additional documentation burden.
