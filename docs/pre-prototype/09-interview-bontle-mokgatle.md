## Interview details

* Participant: Bontle Mokgatle
* Role: Apex Dynamics business sponsor
* Interviewer: Kadijah Batlouni, Apex service designer
* Date: February 20, 2030
* Duration: 27 minutes

## Transcript excerpt

**Kadijah:** What decision must the June steering review make?

**Bontle:** Whether the early warning network is ready for a funded integration program.
The agencies already believe they have a data problem. We need to show that Apex can
turn the feeds into something operational leaders understand.

**Kadijah:** What evidence would be persuasive?

**Bontle:** A recognizable incident unfolding from detection through response. Reviewers
should see signals arrive, understand the threat, locate the kaiju, and see that the
organization can act.

**Kadijah:** Some stakeholders are warning that the inputs remain uncertain.

**Bontle:** Good. We should not claim production accuracy. At the same time, if the first
five minutes of the demo are caveats, we will lose the room. The artifact needs a clear
story.

**Kadijah:** Which audience matters most?

**Bontle:** Incident leadership for credibility and agency executives for funding. The
same screen may not serve both in production, but we cannot build two concepts before
June.

**Kadijah:** What would make the prototype feel credible to them?

**Bontle:** A map, an obvious threat posture, active kaiju details, resource status, and a
visible stream of evidence. It should feel like a command center, not an analytics
notebook.

**Kadijah:** What would make the prototype fail as a learning artifact?

**Bontle:** If people only comment on the colors and layout. I know a polished demo
invites that, but a rough sketch will not unlock the next budget.

**Kadijah:** That is a real tension.

**Bontle:** It is. We need enough polish to earn attention and enough honesty to avoid
implying readiness.

**Kadijah:** Any other requests?

**Bontle:** One small thing. The communications team has been calling the classifier
"Clipzilla." I would love a friendly kaiju mascot in the header that changes expression
when the threat increases. The steering group will remember it, and frankly the program
needs something less grim.

**Kadijah:** Which user need would that address?

**Bontle:** None that I can defend from the interviews. Call it a sponsor preference. If
it threatens the schedule, drop it. If it takes an afternoon, I think it gives the demo
personality.

**Kadijah:** What is non-negotiable?

**Bontle:** The prototype must make fragmentation visible as one coherent scenario. It
must also say plainly that the data is replayed and the connections are mocked.

## Researcher note

Bontle prioritizes a fundable narrative and acknowledges the conflict between
demonstration polish and research fidelity. The mascot request is explicitly a
preference with no user-evidence claim.
