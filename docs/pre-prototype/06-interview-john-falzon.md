## Interview details

* Participant: John Falzon
* Role: Field responder representative
* Interviewer: Kadijah Batlouni, Apex service designer
* Date: February 15, 2030
* Duration: 28 minutes

## Transcript excerpt

**Kadijah:** When does the regional picture help you?

**John:** Before deployment, if it tells us what we are heading into. During movement,
local conditions age faster than the regional picture. After arrival, we may be the
best source and the worst connected source at the same time.

**Kadijah:** Describe a time the official picture was wrong.

**John:** The predicted track was reasonable. The route was not. A seawall failure
put water across the marked approach, and a stalled evacuation bus blocked the
alternative. Dispatch kept asking our estimated arrival because the map still showed a
clear road.

**Kadijah:** How did you correct it?

**John:** Radioed our branch. The branch told command. Someone updated a chat. I do
not know whether the route system ever changed.

**Kadijah:** What information do you need before accepting an assignment?

**John:** Target, objective, known hazards, route, fallback, communications plan, and
whether the unit has the right equipment. "Heavy team available" does not answer those
questions.

**Kadijah:** Who should be able to change an assignment?

**John:** Command sets intent. The unit leader can refuse an unsafe route or report
that the unit cannot perform the task. That is not insubordination. It is part of the
control loop.

**Kadijah:** How do you feel about direct dispatch from a shared display?

**John:** Concerned. A button makes action look immediate. Between a command decision
and movement are acknowledgment, briefing, route confirmation, and sometimes another
agency's approval.

**Kadijah:** Could a visual resource count still be useful?

**John:** For planning, yes, if everyone remembers it is a count, not readiness. In a
demo, people will press the button and think the unit moved.

**Kadijah:** What should a prototype represent about field teams?

**John:** That we can add evidence, not only receive orders. Show what happens when a
field report contradicts the model. If the concept only flows from sensors to command
to us, it is missing half the response.

**Kadijah:** What would success look like?

**John:** Fewer assignments based on conditions that stopped being true.

## Researcher note

John views action as a negotiated, multi-step commitment rather than a command-center
button. He values the regional picture when field corrections can change it.
