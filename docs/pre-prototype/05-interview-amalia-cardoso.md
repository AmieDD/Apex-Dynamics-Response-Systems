## Interview details

* Participant: Amalia Cardoso
* Role: Data and communications operations representative
* Interviewer: Kadijah Batlouni, Apex service designer
* Date: February 14, 2030
* Duration: 39 minutes

## Transcript excerpt

**Kadijah:** What does the early warning network know well?

**Amalia:** It knows that something changed. It is less consistent at knowing what
changed. The network was assembled quickly from systems designed for weather, shipping,
seismic monitoring, defense, utilities, and transportation.

**Kadijah:** How does a report become a named kaiju?

**Amalia:** Sometimes a source names it. Sometimes an analyst associates a track with
the registry. Sometimes software groups observations by proximity and movement. Those
paths can look identical after processing.

**Kadijah:** What is the consequence?

**Amalia:** A polished roster can imply discrete, known entities when the source data is
still a set of possible associations. That does not mean a roster is useless. It means
the transformation carries judgment.

**Kadijah:** What does the registry actually know about each kaiju? Which ones we have
seen, whether they move on land, in water, or in the air, how fast they are, how
heavy?

**Amalia:** We have some of that. There are entries with estimates for size, speed, and
how they tend to move. I would not promise that all of it is accurate. Some figures came
from a single sighting, some were revised after later events, and some were copied
between systems without anyone checking the original.

**Kadijah:** Could Apex use those figures in a prototype?

**Amalia:** You can use them. Just do not present them as more settled than they are.

**Kadijah:** What do people misunderstand about freshness?

**Amalia:** They see a live clock and assume the whole display is live. Each source has
a different observation interval, transport delay, and correction process. "Updated
now" may mean a record was processed now for an observation made earlier.

**Kadijah:** Would a confidence percentage help?

**Amalia:** Help whom? A percentage can summarize model behavior, source agreement, or
analyst confidence. Those are different. If we combine them, the number becomes
decorative precision.

**Kadijah:** What do you want command to see?

**Amalia:** Enough provenance to know when to ask a question. I do not want every schema
field on the main screen. I also do not want uncertainty hidden because it is hard to
visualize.

**Kadijah:** What does data silence mean?

**Amalia:** Nothing by itself. The kaiju may have left the coverage area. The sensor may
have failed. Communications may be down. Some kaiju interfere with electronics, so the
most dangerous event can remove its own evidence.

**Kadijah:** Apex has a replay sample. What can it demonstrate?

**Amalia:** That we can parse those particular records, associate some of them, and show
an interpretation. It cannot demonstrate live reliability, universal thresholds, or
that the association rule works on another incident.

**Kadijah:** What would you push back on in a prototype?

**Amalia:** A single threat condition with no path back to the evidence. Automatic
merging that erases competing tracks. Treating missing as zero. Calling generated
summaries confirmed.

**Kadijah:** Is there anything you would support despite those risks?

**Amalia:** Yes. Put the feeds together. Let us see whether people can detect the
conflict faster. I am against false certainty, not synthesis.

## Researcher note

Amalia supports aggregation but challenges the appearance of discrete identity,
freshness, and precision. She frames every derived value as a transformation that
needs an accountable meaning.
